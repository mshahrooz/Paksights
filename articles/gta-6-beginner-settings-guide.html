GTA 6 beginner settings: controls, camera, subtitles and accessibility.

A spoiler-free starting checklist based on Rockstar’s official options, not mystery ‘perfect settings’.

Before your first long session, take a few minutes to visit Settings. Rockstar documents a useful range of controls and comfort options, including features that are easy to miss.
✓
VERIFIED SOURCE POLICY

Claims below are anchored to Rockstar, Take-Two or documented platform information. Our interpretations are clearly distinguished from official statements.

01/ Start with readability

Open the game’s Settings menu and look for the user-interface and accessibility categories. Rockstar documents UI scaling and subtitle options. Pick text and interface sizes you can comfortably read from your normal viewing distance. A large TV across the room often needs different settings from a monitor at a desk.

02/ Check camera comfort early

Rockstar lists camera-comfort settings. Rather than copying a stranger’s suggested values, adjust the camera to reduce discomfort during movement and driving. Make one change at a time and try it in a safe area before altering multiple options.

03/ Fine-tune aim and look

The supported settings include aim and look sensitivity and inverted controls. Begin with familiar controller behavior and adjust gradually. On PlayStation 5, the documentation also mentions DualSense-related options.

04/ Use available accessibility tools

High-contrast shaders, narration and subtitle settings are identified by Rockstar Support. Explore them if you have visual, auditory or reading preferences. A feature being available does not mean there is one setting that works best for everyone.

05/ Driving, aircraft, bicycles and more

The controls guide covers on-foot movement, melee, vehicles, aircraft, bicycles, swimming and parachuting. Consider checking the relevant mapping before a difficult sequence instead of guessing during active gameplay.

06/ What this guide does not claim

We are not declaring a universal best sensitivity, a guaranteed frame-rate fix or a hidden performance boost. Those require actual controlled testing and depend on hardware, display, preference and game updates.
