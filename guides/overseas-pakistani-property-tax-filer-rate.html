Overseas Pakistani property tax relief: who can use filer rates?

What FBR says about Sections 236C and 236K for eligible NICOP/POC holders.

PakSights research deskPublished 10 Nov 2026 · Sources reviewed 10 Nov 2026
In brief

FBR says certain non-resident overseas Pakistanis holding NICOP or POC can receive filer-rate advance income tax on property purchases and sales under Sections 236K and 236C even if they are not otherwise filers, subject to its published conditions and verification process.

What you should know first

This is a specific transaction benefit, not a general exemption from every tax. FBR’s published FAQ ties it to NICOP/POC ownership and non-resident status, and describes a registrar or housing society initiating a payment slip for commissioner approval.

Tax slabs and rates change when finance legislation changes. Because older online examples can outlive the law, use the current FBR schedule at the date of transfer rather than relying on a copied percentage.

Step-by-step: what to do

Work through these actions in order. The official service may ask for additional information depending on your circumstances.

01

Confirm your documents

Ensure your NICOP or POC is valid and correctly linked to your identity.

02

Check the stated non-residence condition

FBR’s property FAQ specifies the relevant overseas non-residence criterion. Prepare documentary support for the applicable tax year.

03

Tell the recording authority in advance

Before the transfer date, ask the registrar, development authority or housing society how it processes the overseas-filer-rate request.

04

Prepare documentary evidence

FBR describes uploading NICOP/POC details, a scan of the card and information supporting non-residence.

05

Allow for approval

The authority creates the PSID and the request is reviewed through FBR’s Commissioner approval process before payment at the eligible rate.

06

Ask for the whole tax estimate

Check withholding, capital gains, provincial duties and other transfer costs separately with a tax adviser or relevant authority.

Common mistakes to avoid

Look out for these
  • “Filer rate” does not mean “no tax”.
  • The property-specific FBR definition should not be substituted for every other tax-residency test.
  • Never pay an agent to “switch your filer status” without official confirmation.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to be on the Active Taxpayers List?

FBR provides a special filer-rate procedure for qualifying non-resident NICOP/POC holders even when they are non-filers.

Does this apply to both buying and selling?

FBR identifies purchase tax under 236K and sale tax under 236C.

Can I use an old tax-rate table?

Do not rely on an old rate table; confirm the latest law and the transaction value band.
