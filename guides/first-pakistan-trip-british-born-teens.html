Family & Kids

Helping British-born teenagers enjoy their first Pakistan visit

Give teenagers a role in planning while respecting family priorities and cultural differences.

PakSights editorial desk · Published 11 November 2026 · Research checked 11 November 2026

In brief: Give teenagers a role in planning while respecting family priorities and cultural differences. Use the sections below as a flexible planning framework and check official information before making decisions.

Let young people shape some activities

Ask each teenager to suggest interests such as music, museums, sport, food or family photography. Match a small number of activities to these interests, while keeping safety guidance and transport in mind.

Discuss expectations ahead of time

Explain where they will stay, who they will meet and what social occasions may involve. Talk about clothing, privacy, screen time and household rules openly instead of waiting until everyone is tired.

Make connections personal

Invite relatives to share stories and photos. A teenager who is not fluent in Urdu or a regional language can still build relationships through everyday activities, humor, cooking and shared interests.

Allow downtime

Long journeys and frequent gatherings can overwhelm a teenager. Set aside space for rest and communication with friends while balancing family time. Let them ask uncomfortable questions without embarrassment.

Keep this checklist handy

Two personal interests per teen; family event calendar; household expectations; internet plan; downtime; local safety information.

Frequently asked question

Must teenagers already speak Urdu?

No. Encourage everyday phrases and let conversations develop naturally.
