Easy Urdu phrases children can use with grandparents

Practical Roman Urdu for greetings, everyday needs and friendly conversations.

PakSights editorial desk · Published 11 November 2026 · Research checked 11 November 2026

In brief: Practical Roman Urdu for greetings, everyday needs and friendly conversations. Use the sections below as a flexible planning framework and check official information before making decisions.

Make language useful, not a test

Pick phrases children can use right away and practice them in everyday situations. Family language may be Punjabi, Sindhi, Pashto or another language rather than Urdu, and that is equally worth celebrating.

Start with greetings

Assalam-o-alaikum is a common greeting. Aap kaisay hain? means 'How are you?' in polite everyday speech. Shukriya means 'Thank you.' Practice a friendly tone rather than memorizing long lists.

Add practical phrases

Mujhay pani chahiye means 'I would like water.' Mujhay bhook lagi hai means 'I am hungry.' Mujhay madad chahiye means 'I need help.' Expressions vary by region, family and pronunciation.

Practice with relatives

Role-play greeting a grandparent, asking about their day or thanking someone after a meal. Invite the family to add their own words and explain differences without correcting children harshly.

Keep this checklist handy

Three greetings; two phrases for needs; a short role-play; a family language notebook; one weekly conversation.

Frequently asked question

Is this a formal Urdu course?

No. It is a simple starting point for conversation and should be adapted to your family's language.
