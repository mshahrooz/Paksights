Pakistan tax residency for overseas Pakistanis: questions to check

Don’t rely solely on the popular “183-day rule” when analyzing tax residency.

PakSights research deskPublished 10 Nov 2026 · Sources reviewed 10 Nov 2026
In brief

Pakistan’s Income Tax Ordinance section 82 sets out conditions for resident-individual status. These include 183-day presence and other statutory tests, so physical days in Pakistan are not the only possible consideration.

What you should know first

A person’s immigration status, citizenship and tax residency are different concepts. Tax status can also differ between Pakistan and the country where you live.

Section 82 published by FBR includes a 120-days-plus-prior-four-years test and a provision for certain government employees posted abroad. Rules may be affected by subsequent amendments and tax treaties; use a current tax-year review.

Step-by-step: what to do

Work through these actions in order. The official service may ask for additional information depending on your circumstances.

01

Identify the relevant Pakistani tax year

Make sure you are counting days under the tax-year definition used for the return or transaction in question.

02

Create a travel log

Use passport stamps, airline records and a calendar to record physical presence accurately. Keep entry and exit dates.

03

Check every Section 82 test

Review the complete current wording, not merely the 183-day threshold. Government-posting and prior-year presence can change results.

04

Separate foreign and Pakistan-source income

List income sources and assets before assuming that non-residency means no Pakistan filing or payment obligations.

05

Review any applicable treaty

If two countries may regard you as tax resident, get advice on the relevant double-taxation agreement and reporting obligations.

06

Keep transaction-specific evidence

Property transfers, bank onboarding and tax filings may ask for proof of non-resident status under their own procedures.

Common mistakes to avoid

Look out for these
  • The FBR property-tax relief rule uses a specific non-residence condition; do not assume it settles general annual tax residency.
  • A 183-day shortcut can be incomplete under Section 82.
  • This guide cannot determine anyone’s tax liability without a full fact pattern.

Frequently asked questions

Am I non-resident if I spent fewer than 183 days in Pakistan?

Not necessarily for every purpose. Read all applicable Section 82 tests and any transaction-specific rule.

Does holding NICOP determine tax residency?

No. Identity-document status and tax-residency criteria are different.

Can I be tax resident in two countries?

Potentially yes under domestic rules; treaties and professional assessment can affect the outcome.
